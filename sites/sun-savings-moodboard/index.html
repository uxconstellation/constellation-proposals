Sun Savings website rebuild Prepared for VirtuHire Direction sign-off, 5 August 2026

Everyone else makes you knock. We leave the light on.

The direction in one line: a lit instrument on a dark night. Sun Savings becomes the one solar company in South Africa that gives you your number before it asks who you are, and the page is built so that the answer is the brightest thing on it.

Why this direction

Four competitors, one shared habit

We captured and tore down every serious player in SA residential solar. Two patterns hold across all of them, and both are openings.

Habit one: they all sell you the sky

Every captured competitor leads on daylight. Blue sky behind panels at Alumo and LookSee, a saturated yellow field at GoSolr, a bright interior at Wetility. The sky is the least ownable image in the category because all four already own it.

Our move: no sky, and no photograph in the hero at all. A dark ground, type, and a lit instrument. It is the only hero in the captured set that does not open on daylight.

Habit two: the number is the reward for a lead

Alumo gates a page literally called Sizing Tool behind a four field form. Wetility asks for your address before it will recommend. GoSolr asks where you live before it tells you anything. Sun Savings' own funnel is a contact form.

Our move: answer first. The calculator sits in the hero, un-gated, and the lead capture happens only after the visitor has seen their own number.

The two references behind every decision on this board

We are not inventing this. We are rebuilding two real pages

What we take from Wise

  • The calculator is the hero, and it answers before it asks.
  • A ledger, not a headline number. Labelled lines with values make a financial figure read as audited rather than marketed.
  • A guarantee with a duration printed inside the instrument. Wise prints "guaranteed for 47h" on the rate. Sun Savings already promises 0% escalation fixed for 60 months and currently buries it in a bullet list. It belongs in the card.
  • A qualifier chip plus one sentence saying why. Wise explains its volume discount. We explain why your bill band qualifies you for a tier.
  • The action lives inside the answer card.

Measured on the capture: 550px calculator card in a 1440px frame, 627 flex containers against 14 grid, zero canvas elements, 62 inline SVG. It is ordinary DOM, which is why it survives a React rebuild on Lovable and stays inside the LCP budget.

The best thing on the current site is hidden

The page is titled CALCULATOR and it is not in the navigation. It is reachable from the sitemap and from search, and from nowhere else on your own site.

This direction is not foreign to Sun Savings. It is the Sun Savings calculator page, promoted to the front door and rebuilt properly. The model, the inputs and the output fields already exist and are already approved in substance. We are re-expressing them, not inventing them.

Three defects we fix in the rebuild: it is orphaned out of the nav, it renders R0,00 placeholders before you touch it, and the King package's hardware inside the calculator contradicts the King sold everywhere else on the site.

Why a beam, and why it is ours

GoSolr already prints a static light beam over its selected plan on a black ground. We captured it, so we know exactly what we are deviating from.

  • Ours is driven by the bill slider, so the light does the choosing in front of you rather than decorating a choice you already made.
  • Ours is white light on night blue rather than brand yellow, so the light reads as light and the green stays reserved for what you save.
  • It earns the name. A solar company whose page is lit by a moving shaft of light, on a country's worth of shared experience of the lights going off.
  • It is cheap. One absolutely positioned element, a clip-path wedge over a linear gradient, animated on transform only. No canvas, no WebGL, no library beyond GSAP, nothing that touches layout.
Handoff to motion-lead

The motion spec, precisely

TriggerBehaviour
Slider inputgsap.quickTo on the beam's x, 0.45s, power3.out. The beam tracks continuously while dragging, it does not wait for release.
Tier changeTimeline: beam settles 0.45s, then the lit card's hairline border draws 0.3s power2.out, then five ledger rows stagger in at 0.06s on y:8 and opacity, then each numeric value counts to its figure over 0.6s power2.out with snap to integer. Total about 1.0s.
Scroll into pageScrollTrigger, scrub:true, over the first 40vh: the shaft rotates from 18 degrees to 6 degrees and lengthens, so the light appears to rise as you enter. No pinning.
Echo, two thirds downThe same shaft crosses the single dark proof band once on enter. One echo only. It is not a page-wide effect.

Rules the motion must hold

  • The light has an edge and a direction. It is a clip-path wedge, never a soft radial bloom sitting behind a card. A blurred glow on a dark background is the single most common tell of generated design and we do not ship it.
  • Transform, opacity and clip-path only. Nothing that reflows, so CLS stays under 0.1.
  • Reduced motion: the beam snaps to position, values render final, rows appear without stagger. The answer is never withheld for the sake of an animation.
  • The proof gate: a fast drag and a fast scroll, captured in a real browser, asserting the lit tier and the final ledger values. Motion nobody watched does not ship.

Aktiv Grotesk is not ours to ship

Your current site rents Aktiv Grotesk through Squarespace's Adobe Fonts entitlement. That licence does not travel to a new build. We replace it with a face we can license cleanly and self host.

Archivo, by Omnibus-Type

  • Licence: SIL Open Font License 1.1. Free to use commercially, free to self host, no per-domain fee, and it transfers with the site if ownership ever changes hands.
  • One variable file, 88 KB for the whole latin set, covering every weight from 100 to 900 and every width from 62 to 125. One request, which protects the under 2.5 second load target the current site misses.
  • It has true tabular figures. We inspected the font binary: the tnum feature is present, so every rand figure in the ledger sits on the same column and the numbers hold still as they count up. That steadiness is what makes a column of figures read as a record.
  • It is not what the category uses. Alumo and your own calculator page are set in Poppins, Wetility in Open Sans. Archivo's engineered, slightly squared grotesque reads closer to a meter reading than to a brochure.

The one idea

We use a single family and let its width axis carry the contrast: headlines run wide and confident, ledger labels run narrow and uppercase like the small print on a utility statement. Wise gets that separation by paying for two typefaces. We get it from one file.

Sizes hold Wise's measured display to body ratio of 3.25 at 3.5: display 40 to 64px, section heads 32 to 44px, body 17 to 18px, ledger labels 12px. Three weights only, 400, 600 and 700, matching the discipline of your current site which uses just two.

The rules that come with it

  • Green fills shapes and never sets text. At 2.06:1 on white it cannot legally carry a word. It fills buttons and chips with night text on top, and within the calculator it is reserved for the saving.
  • Your brand blue never carries body text on white. It measures 3.11:1. Your current site uses it for links, which fails AA today. The deep blue at 6.93:1 takes that job.
  • The ground colour is also the ink. One value doing both jobs is the restraint device we measured on Wise, where the forest green appears 262 times as text and 11 times as a background.
  • Two greens get deleted. rgb(3,228,82) and rgb(64,205,2) come off the site.

Spacing and rhythm

An eight point grid, and the page goes dark twice

The scale

Wise's measured gaps are fractional artefacts of its framework: 4, 8, 9.38, 18.77, 28.15, 37.54 pixels. Your current site has no scale at all, only Squarespace block spacing at 7, 8.27, 11 and 14 pixels. We rationalise both to whole numbers, because a CMS needs a token scale that an editor cannot break.

4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128. Section padding 96 desktop, 64 tablet, 48 mobile.

The frame

  • 1440 page frame, 1200 content column, 12 columns, 24 gutter.
  • Hero splits 6 columns of persuasion against 5 of instrument. The instrument card is 550px, measured off the Wise capture where the same card is 550px in a 1440 frame.
  • Body text capped at 68 characters and headings at 20, which is what holds every heading to a ceiling of three lines at any screen width.
  • Mobile first. The instrument stacks above the persuasion copy on small screens, because on a phone the answer should be the first thing on the page.

Imagery

There is no honest photograph of Sun Savings on the internet

We audited all 41 images on your home page. The two heaviest are Pngtree stock, at 907 KB and 476 KB. Several others carry [fpdl.in] in the filename, the signature of a Freepik download proxy. There is not one photograph of a real Sun Savings installation anywhere on the home page.

We will not carry that across, and we will not generate replacements with AI. Provenance we cannot evidence is a liability we would be handing to the client, and invented imagery of installations you did not do is not something we are willing to put our name to.

The direction absorbs this rather than stalling on it

The hero deliberately has no photograph. Its largest painted element is type on the night ground, which means the page's load performance does not depend on an image we do not have. The site can launch on schedule with zero new photography. Photography then lands in the proof sections, where it can arrive later without holding up anything.

Treatment, when it does arrive

  • Dusk and interior, never midday. Cool shadows toward the night blue, warm light where the power is. It is the opposite of the bright sky every competitor uses, and it is the only lighting that shows what the product is actually for.
  • Photographs never carry type. Text sits in an adjacent solid block, so we never need a scrim or a shadow to make it legible.
  • Real hardware, real hands, real houses, photographed working rather than posed, which rules out the stock gesture of a model pointing at a panel.

Discipline

What this direction will not do

A short list, because most of what makes a site look generated is a set of habits rather than a single decision.

  • No drop shadows. Not on cards, not on buttons, not on text. Separation comes from value, a hairline and a change of surface. Wise floats its calculator card on a shadow, and that is the one part of its craft we are choosing not to take.
  • No glow. The light in this design has an edge and a direction. A blurred coloured bloom behind an element on a dark background is the most common tell of AI generated design.
  • No gradient text. Headlines are one colour.
  • No 01 / 02 / 03 eyebrows above section headings.
  • No AI imagery, ever, and none of the current stock library carries over.
  • No third green, and no fourth phone number. One green, one number, one price per product.
  • No midday sky. Four competitors already own it.
  • No heading longer than three lines, at any screen width.
  • No motion we have not watched. The signature moment is prototyped in isolation and reviewed on video before it enters the build.

Signing off this board

This is a direction round, not a revision round. Approving it does not consume any of the design revisions in the agreement. What we need is a yes, a no, or a note on each of five things: the one line direction, the type, the colour, the calculator treatment, and the signature moment.
