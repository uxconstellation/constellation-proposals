01 · The Platform

The full QA lifecycle,
one system of record.

The five phases below are your specification's own, in your own order, with the step counts it defines. They run off shared registers: testers, sites, PT panels, certificates and lots. The rules that govern them, the HIV RT algorithm, the PT scoring rubric, panel structure, renewal windows and checklist templates, are configuration a Super User edits, not code a developer redeploys. Your rubric is mid-change already, with two scoring areas being phased out and one added, and the panel structure is moving from four panels of three samples to two of five. A system that needs a developer for either is the wrong system.

Phase 1 · Newly trained testers, ten steps

The First 10 EQA samples, captured on Template 1 or uploaded from paper, scored automatically against the HIV RT algorithm at the 80% pass mark. A pass generates the authorisation letter and certificate; a fail opens the retest path, including a partial batch that gets added to, or a larger resubmission. The requisition barcode is captured against the submission even though the physical matching stays manual.

Phase 2 · PT certification, seven steps

Panels are registered with their predetermined target results before a round opens, and the number of panels and samples per panel is configurable, not hardcoded. Static-site and door-to-door forms both feed one record. Scoring runs the rubric out of twenty, at the same 80% mark, and auto-sorts the round into pass and fail.

Phase 3 · Expiry and renewal, five steps

Every tester and site certificate carries an issue and expiry date as a permanent attribute of the record, not a filed document. Two months out, the renewal list surfaces on screen, filterable by region, site and days to expiry, and by email. Where a passed PT in the last twelve months is missing, the system routes back into a fresh Phase 2 round rather than renewing against a gap.

Phase 4 · New site readiness, nine steps

The Template 6 checklist across its six categories, completed on a device on site or on paper and scanned back. A failed item opens a corrective-action loop that tracks exactly which items failed, notifies the site, and logs the re-inspection against the same assessment record, as many cycles as it takes. Then verification testing, scored on the same algorithm, auto-flags the site as ready.

Phase 5 · Post-market surveillance, four steps

Old kit against new kit across twenty samples, concordant or discordant per sample, and acceptable or unacceptable per sample. Lot numbers and expiry dates stay free text by design, because the lot pipeline with MoHSS and medical stores is not yet mature enough to validate against a list. The report compiles and emails to MoHSS and stakeholders from the system.

Across all five · Status, windows and audit

A status dashboard per phase, so an administrator never opens records one at a time to see where a batch stands, plus search across every tester and site by status. One notification engine, not five: a thirty-day default window, extendable per tester, site or round, a ten-day reminder to the tester and a two-day list to administrators. Every create, update, approve and status change is logged with user, timestamp and before and after values.

Their accounts from day one

Hosting, database and domain are created under accounts the client controls, with billing in their name. We work as operators inside them, never as the owner they have to ask.

06 · The Technical Breakdown

Standard pieces,
none of them proprietary.

Nothing here is a black box. If the client ever changes teams, they hold a standard PostgreSQL database and a standard React codebase under their own accounts, not an export from a platform they do not own.

Frontend

React + TypeScript

The console, dashboards and every workflow screen as an ordinary React codebase. Editable by any development team, not locked to one tool or one vendor.

Backend

Cloudflare + PostgreSQL

Cloudflare for the application edge and the domain, as recommended on the call: it is fast in-region, cheap at this scale, and hardened against the traffic a public-facing government-adjacent system attracts. PostgreSQL underneath, the most portable database there is. Every account is opened in the client's name, so nothing here routes through us.

Business rules

Configuration, not code

The HIV RT algorithm, the PT rubric, panel and sample counts, testing windows, dropdown lists and notification rules are all data a Super User edits. This is not a nice-to-have: your rubric is mid-change, with two scoring areas phased out and one added, and the panel structure is moving from four panels of three to two of five. Any of that requiring a developer would be a design failure.

Documents

Generated, signed, versioned

Certificates and reports generate as PDF from record data. Electronic signature writes a full audit entry; a printed wet-signature route exists wherever the programme requires one.

Paper is a first-class path

Connectivity is not reliable everywhere the programme runs, so every form downloads blank in its original layout, comes back on paper, and is entered by an administrator against the same record. The choice is per submission, not a setting per tester, because the same person may submit digitally one round and on paper the next. The resulting record is identical either way, bar an entered-by note in the audit trail.

The architecture document

Delivered as part of the engagement, not sold back to you afterwards: data model, security model, hosting topology, third-party dependencies with their licences, and the compliance controls and design decisions behind them. It is what lets a different team pick this up without us, which is the whole point of the ownership principle.

Security

POPIA-grade by default

Encrypted personal information, role isolation, an append-only hash-chained audit trail, and an external-access sweep before go-live. Data stays in the client's infrastructure.

Ownership

Theirs from day one

The client's Supabase project, the client's hosting, the client's domain. Standard tooling underneath, nothing to migrate later, source transferred on completion.

The one thing that has to be right

A PT score decides whether a person keeps testing patients, so the scoring path is engineered like the safety system it is: results are scored against versioned criteria, every score keeps the criteria version it was scored under, borderline and failed results route to a named human reviewer, and the audit trail is append-only, so nothing in the system can be silently edited or deleted. That is what an accreditation review will ask for, and it has to be designed in on day one, not retrofitted.

It is also the honest answer to whether this could run without a purpose-built application. A no-code stack can capture forms, but it cannot give you versioned scoring rules, role isolation enforced at the data layer and an audit trail that survives scrutiny. The purpose-built core is deliberately small; everything likely to change lives in configuration, which is where the flexibility you asked for actually comes from.

07 · Where AI Earns Its Place

AI where it saves hours,
never where it signs.

You asked where AI adds meaningful value. Our answer: in the workflows around the system of record, never inside its verdicts. Scores, certificates and audit entries stay deterministic and reviewable. AI removes the manual hours that surround them, every AI-assisted step is confirmed by a person, and every one of those confirmations lands in the same audit trail.

Intelligent document intake

Training certificates, identity documents and signed forms arrive as scans. AI reads them, extracts the fields, matches them to the tester record and flags mismatches for a person to confirm, instead of a coordinator retyping every one.

Migration cleanup

The SharePoint export will carry duplicates, naming drift and orphaned records; every legacy migration does. AI-assisted matching proposes the merges and corrections, and a person approves each one before it lands in the new registers.

Validation at the point of capture

Results that break the configured algorithm's expected pattern, impossible dates, out-of-range values, a pass recorded against a failed control, are flagged at entry, before they reach scoring, not discovered in an audit.

Plain-language reporting

Ask the dashboard a question, which sites are at risk this quarter, and get the answer with the records behind it. Stakeholder packs draft themselves from live data instead of a week of collation.

Renewal and PT correspondence

Notices, reminders and escalations drafted per tester and per site in the programme's own tone, queued for a person to send. Nothing leaves the system silently.

In-app user assistance

An assistant that answers how-do-I questions from the system's own documentation and the programme's SOPs, which cuts training time and support load as new supervisors come on board.

On doing this without a purpose-built application

You asked whether the requirement could be met without a hard-coded application, and the question deserves a straight answer. We build with AI heavily, it is why the MVP costs what it does, and the assistants above are real, bounded AI features. What we do not recommend is running certification itself on a no-code or prompt-generated stack: PT scoring, role isolation and an append-only audit trail have to be engineered and tested once, deliberately, or the programme inherits a system it cannot defend. The core we build is deliberately small and everything likely to change is configuration, which delivers the flexibility you were reaching for without giving up the auditability.
